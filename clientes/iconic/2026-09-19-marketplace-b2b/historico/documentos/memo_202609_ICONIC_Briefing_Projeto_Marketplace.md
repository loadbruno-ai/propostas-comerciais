## 1. Resumo da reunião

### Contexto e estratégia

- Marketplace B2B white label para o setor de aftermarket automotivo (carros, motos, caminhões).
- Marca nova, confidencial, sem vínculo visível com Ipiranga, Texaco ou ICONIC.
- Público-alvo: pequenos varejistas (CNPJ), não consumidor final.
- Mercado hoje extremamente fragmentado, sem portal online consolidado para o setor.
- Lançamento por estado, não nacional simultâneo — início em Santa Catarina, Minas Gerais e Rio de Janeiro.
  - Cada estado leva ~3 meses para conectar estoques, tabelas de preço e frete.
- Plataforma de e-commerce ainda em definição (fase Bridge); dois fornecedores em avaliação, decisão nos próximos 15 dias. Front-end vem junto com a plataforma escolhida.
- Programa de fidelidade existente (offline) a ser migrado para a plataforma futuramente.
- Fase 1: adesão e primeira compra. Fase 2: clube de compras, cashback, cupons, recompra.

### Desafios e necessidades

- Projeto confidencial, pouquíssimas pessoas envolvidas, sem time de digital interno (sem analista de tráfego, sem criação de arte, sem estratégia de CRM montada).
- Contratação prevista do lado ICONIC apenas para área comercial (trazer fornecedores).
- Dois perfis de base a trabalhar:
  - Base inativa dos distribuidores → ação via CRM intensivo.
  - Base nova (varejistas desconhecidos) → awareness + scraping/prospecção por CNPJ.
- Barreiras de adesão dos distribuidores: resistência em ceder base de clientes e pagar comissão.
- CRM: nenhuma ferramenta definida ainda, tudo a contratar.
- Prazo apertado: início de execução em dezembro/2026–janeiro/2027 (3 meses para fechar agência e contrato).

## 2. Detailed scope implied by her request

*(Draft de escopo — interpretação do que a Camila sinalizou como necessário, para orientar a construção da proposta)*

### Strategic foundation

- Define the new brand's launch narrative without using ICONIC, Ipiranga, or Texaco as visible endorsements.
- Clarify the value proposition for small retailers: a B2B place to buy products for car, motorcycle, and truck businesses.
- Translate the project into a phased roadmap: awareness → registration → first purchase → repeat purchase → loyalty.

### Go-to-market by state

- Build a launch playbook for Santa Catarina, Minas Gerais, and Rio de Janeiro.
- Establish what must be ready before each launch: connected distributor inventory, price tables, freight, product assortment, campaign assets, CRM flows, and measurement.
- Avoid high media investment where the assortment is still too limited to be attractive to retailers.
- Treat each rollout as a learning cycle before expanding into more states.

### Awareness and demand generation

- Create a plan to reach retailers who do not know an online purchasing portal is available to them.
- Build campaigns across Instagram, Google/search, paid traffic, and other digital channels.
- Develop messaging around practical purchase needs—not just brand awareness—including products such as tires, batteries, parts, wiper blades, and filters.
- Consider future physical activations only after the initial digital launch; offline is not the immediate priority.

### Lead generation and prospecting

- Define which CNPJ profiles are priority prospects.
- Identify ways to map and approach unknown retailers rather than relying solely on purchased lead lists.
- Structure the path from prospect identification to campaign exposure, CNPJ registration, and first order.
- Segment outreach by geography, retailer type, and purchasing potential. *(Os últimos critérios de segmentação são uma forma recomendada de operacionalizar a necessidade dela, não algo que ela definiu explicitamente.)*

### CRM and lifecycle activation

- Help choose the CRM solution, since nothing has been selected.
- Design CRM journeys for distributor-provided inactive or low-frequency customer bases.
- Build communications for:
  - platform announcement;
  - registration;
  - first purchase;
  - product/category discovery;
  - promotion;
  - repurchase.
- Plan how CRM activity will coexist with distributors' concerns about customer-base ownership and commissions.

### Conversion and e-commerce experience

- Work with the selected e-commerce platform team on on-site conversion requirements.
- Define campaign placements and assets: banners, pop-ups, promotional pages, and product-led communications.
- Ensure the platform supports the commercial calendar and future promotional mechanics.
- Treat front-end design as primarily covered by the platform implementation, while the agency focuses on marketing activation and conversion needs.

### Creative and operating capacity

- Provide the creative production ICONIC does not have internally: campaign visuals, performance creatives, CRM pieces, banners, and platform content.
- Supply ongoing digital marketing capacity because the internal hiring plan is focused on commercial roles that will bring suppliers and products into the marketplace.
- Coordinate closely with commercial stakeholders so marketing activity reflects available assortment and supplier participation.

### Loyalty and retention roadmap

- Treat loyalty as a later phase, after initial adoption and first purchases.
- Explore migration of the current offline program into the platform.
- Evaluate mechanisms such as a buying club, cashback, product discounts, and exclusive coupons.
- Focus on keeping retailers buying through the marketplace instead of returning to other channels.

### What she needs from Fattoria now

- A concrete initial scope, rather than a generic proposal.
- A roadmap with workstreams, priorities, milestones, required team, and estimated effort.
- A clear split between what ICONIC will own—mainly commercial/supplier expansion—and what the agency would own across strategy, CRM, awareness, media, creative, and activation.
- A proposal calibrated to a constrained budget and a fast decision cycle, with execution expected to start in December 2026 or January 2027.
